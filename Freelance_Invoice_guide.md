# Freelancer's Beginner Guide: Creating an Invoice

> **Note:** This is a coaching guide, not legal or tax advice. Invoice and tax rules (GST, VAT, TDS, etc.) differ by country, so check the rules where you live or ask an accountant once.

---

## What Is an Invoice?

An invoice is a **formal request for payment**. It tells the client what work you did, how much they owe, and how to pay you. It is also your **proof of income**, which you'll need for taxes and records.

A contract says "this is what we agreed." An invoice says "this is what you now owe."

---

## Step 1: Decide When to Invoice

Don't wait until you feel like it. Decide this in your contract. Common ways:

- **Advance invoice:** before starting work (30-50% of the total)
- **Milestone invoice:** after each stage is completed
- **Final invoice:** after delivery, for the remaining balance
- **Monthly invoice:** for ongoing or retainer work
- **Hourly invoice:** at the end of each week or month, with hours listed

> Rule of thumb: send the invoice **the same day** you deliver or hit the milestone. Late invoices mean late payments.

---

## Step 2: Put These Things in Every Invoice

### 1. The Word "Invoice"
Write **INVOICE** clearly at the top so there's no confusion.

### 2. Invoice Number
Every invoice needs a **unique number**. Keep it simple and in order.

- Example: `INV-001`, `INV-002`, `INV-003`
- Or with the year: `2026-001`, `2026-002`

Never reuse a number. This keeps your records clean.

### 3. Invoice Date and Due Date
- **Issue date:** the day you send it
- **Due date:** the last day to pay (for example, 7 or 15 days later)

Write the actual date (like "25 Oct 2026"), not just "within 7 days." A real date is harder to ignore.

### 4. Your Details
- Your full name or business name
- Address
- Email and phone number
- Tax or registration number, if you have one (GST, VAT, PAN, etc.)

### 5. Client Details
- Client's full name or company name
- Billing address
- Email
- Client's tax number, if required

### 6. Description of Work
List each item or service clearly, with quantity and rate.

| Description | Qty / Hours | Rate | Amount |
|---|---|---|---|
| Website design (5 pages) | 1 | 25,000 | 25,000 |
| Extra revision round | 2 hrs | 1,000 | 2,000 |

Be specific. "Logo design, final files delivered" is better than "design work."

### 7. Totals
Show clearly:

- Subtotal
- Discount (if any)
- Tax (if applicable)
- Advance already paid (subtract it)
- **Total amount due**

### 8. Payment Details
Make it very easy to pay you. Include:

- Bank name, account name, account number, IFSC/SWIFT/IBAN
- UPI ID or PayPal email
- Currency

The easier it is to pay, the faster you get paid.

### 9. Payment Terms
A short line like:

> "Payment due within 7 days of invoice date. A late fee of 2% per week applies after the due date."

This should match what's already in your contract.

### 10. Thank-You Note
One friendly line, such as: "Thank you for your business. It was a pleasure working with you." It keeps the relationship warm.

---

## Step 3: Keep It Clean and Professional

- Use a simple layout with plenty of white space
- Use your logo if you have one
- Save and send as a **PDF**, not an editable file
- Name the file clearly: `Invoice-INV-001-ClientName.pdf`

---

## Step 4: Choose Your Tool

You don't need fancy software. Pick one:

- **Free templates:** Word, Google Docs, Canva
- **Free invoicing apps:** Zoho Invoice, Wave, Invoice Ninja
- **Paid tools:** FreshBooks, QuickBooks, Bonsai

Start simple. Upgrade when you have many clients.

---

## Step 5: Send It the Right Way

1. Email the invoice with a short, polite message
2. Mention the amount, the due date, and the payment method in the email body
3. Attach the PDF
4. Ask the client to confirm they received it

**Sample email:**

> Subject: Invoice INV-003 for Website Design - Due 25 Oct 2026
>
> Hi [Client Name],
>
> I hope you're doing well. Please find attached Invoice INV-003 for the website design project.
>
> Amount due: 25,000
> Due date: 25 Oct 2026
> Payment details are included in the invoice.
>
> Please let me know once you've received it. Thank you!
>
> Regards,
> [Your Name]

---

## Step 6: Follow Up on Payment

Don't feel shy. Following up is normal business.

- **Before the due date:** a friendly reminder 1-2 days before
- **On the due date:** a short "payment due today" message
- **After the due date:** a polite follow-up, then mention the late fee
- **Long overdue:** a firm written reminder, pause any ongoing work, and refer to your contract

**Sample reminder:**

> "Hi [Name], just a gentle reminder that Invoice INV-003 was due on 25 Oct. Could you please share an update on the payment? Thank you!"

---

## Step 7: Record and Track Everything

After you get paid:

- Mark the invoice as **Paid** and note the payment date
- Send a payment receipt or a "payment received" message
- Keep a copy of every invoice for at least a few years (check your local tax rules)

A simple tracking sheet is enough:

| Invoice No. | Client | Amount | Issue Date | Due Date | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| INV-001 | ABC Co. | 10,000 | 01 Oct | 08 Oct | Paid |
| INV-002 | XYZ Ltd. | 25,000 | 10 Oct | 17 Oct | Pending |

---

## Common Beginner Mistakes to Avoid

- Delaying the invoice for days or weeks after finishing the work
- Not numbering invoices properly
- Vague descriptions like "work done"
- Forgetting payment details, so the client has to ask
- No due date
- Forgetting to subtract the advance already paid
- Sending editable files instead of PDF
- Not following up because "it feels awkward"
- Not keeping records for tax time

---

## Quick Checklist

- [ ] The word "Invoice" at the top
- [ ] Unique invoice number
- [ ] Issue date and due date
- [ ] Your full details (and tax number if applicable)
- [ ] Client's full details
- [ ] Clear description of work, quantity, rate
- [ ] Subtotal, tax, advance deducted, total due
- [ ] Payment methods and bank/UPI details
- [ ] Payment terms and late fee
- [ ] Thank-you note
- [ ] Saved as PDF with a clear file name
- [ ] Sent by email with a short message
- [ ] Logged in your tracking sheet

---

## Coaching Advice

Send invoices fast, make them clear, and make paying you effortless. Most late payments are not because clients are bad. They happen because the invoice was late, confusing, or hard to pay. Fix those three things and you'll get paid much faster.
